# Sources & References

## Religious Texts & Scripture

### Primary Sources
- *The Holy Bible* (Various translations). Old and New Testaments contain numerous demon references, exorcisms by Jesus, spiritual warfare teachings.
- *The Quran*. Multiple surahs discuss jinn, Iblis, and shayatin. Key passages: Surat Al-Jinn (72), Surat Al-Kahf (18:50), others.
- *The Talmud* (Babylonian and Jerusalem). Jewish oral law and commentary containing demon lore, especially tractates on magical practices.
- *The Torah* and *Tanakh*. Hebrew Bible with references to demons, evil spirits, and protective practices.
- *The Vedas* and *Puranas*. Hindu scriptures describing asuras, rakshasas, and cosmic battles between gods and demons.
- *Buddhist Sutras*. Various texts describing Mara and demon-like entities as obstacles to enlightenment.

## Warning & Ethical Note

Many sources listed present demons as real spiritual entities based on religious conviction. Others provide psychological, sociological, or skeptical explanations. The Wyrdness Project includes diverse perspectives for educational purposes.

**If researching demons for academic purposes**:
- Approach religious texts and believers with respect
- Recognize the deeply personal nature of faith
- Understand the historical and cultural contexts
- Avoid sensationalism or mockery

**If experiencing distressing symptoms you attribute to demons**:
- Seek professional mental health evaluation first
- Consult trusted religious leaders if desired
- Emergency mental health services: National Suicide Prevention Lifeline (988), Crisis Text Line (text HOME to 741741)
